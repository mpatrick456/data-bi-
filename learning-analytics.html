Case study 01 · Learning & Development Analytics

L&D dashboard for compliance, engagement, demand and learning operations follow-up.

This Power BI case study is written from the viewpoint of an L&D data analyst: the aim is to turn learning-system data into trusted insight for HR, compliance owners and operational managers. The dashboard focuses on overdue mandatory learning, learner engagement, course demand, data quality and management follow-up.

Case study summary

Business context and reporting challenge

Learning teams often depend on LMS exports, HR data, course catalogues and manual spreadsheets. The same training record can be interpreted differently depending on status definitions, completion dates, course versioning, employee movement and mandatory-training rules. This creates a reporting risk: managers may see numbers, but still not know where to focus.

The dashboard is designed to solve that problem by separating compliance risk, learner engagement and course effectiveness. It gives stakeholders a clearer view of who is overdue, which courses are creating operational pressure, where data quality needs attention and what follow-up action should happen next.

Stakeholders supported

  • L&D managers monitoring learning health and training demand
  • HR business partners reviewing departmental capability and compliance risk
  • Compliance owners tracking mandatory learning completion
  • Department managers responsible for overdue team members
  • Learning systems analysts managing LMS reporting quality

Problem statement

From learning records to management action

The business problem is not simply “how many people completed training”. The more useful question is: which learner groups, courses and departments need management attention, and what evidence supports that action?

Compliance risk

Identify overdue mandatory learning by department, manager, role or location, then separate urgent long-overdue cases from normal in-progress learning.

Engagement and demand

Analyse attendance, no-shows, cancellation behaviour and course demand so the L&D team can improve scheduling and communications.

Learning value

Connect completion patterns with feedback and course type to identify training that should be redesigned, promoted, scaled or retired.

Dashboard storytelling structure

Page / area Purpose Example insight Business action
Executive overview Provide a single view of learning health for leadership and L&D managers. Completion trend, overdue volume, compliance hotspots and demand changes. Agree which teams, courses or mandatory topics need priority attention.
Compliance monitoring Track mandatory learning completion and ageing of overdue records. A department may appear broadly compliant but still contain a small group of high-risk overdue learners. Target manager follow-up, escalation and learner reminders based on risk.
Engagement and demand Review learner behaviour and operational pressure on courses. High demand with repeated cancellations may indicate poor scheduling, not low learner interest. Adjust course scheduling, communications or capacity planning.
Effectiveness and improvement Use feedback and activity patterns to review learning value. Mandatory courses with weak feedback may need redesign because attendance is compliance-driven. Prioritise course improvement and make evidence-based changes to learning design.

Measures and analysis ideas

  • Completion rate % and overdue learner count
  • Average days overdue and overdue ageing bucket
  • No-show rate %, cancellation rate % and attendance trend
  • Training hours per learner / FTE
  • Average feedback score and course improvement priority
  • Data quality flags for missing learner, course or completion fields
  • Compliance risk category by department, manager and course type

Data professional approach

How I would build and govern the analysis

I would start by clarifying KPI definitions with L&D stakeholders: what counts as completed, overdue, cancelled, no-show and in progress. Then I would structure the data model around learning activity, course, employee and date dimensions, with Power Query used to clean fields, standardise status logic and identify missing or inconsistent records.

The report would then be managed as a repeatable reporting cycle: documented assumptions, refresh checks, reconciliation of totals, data quality monitoring, stakeholder review and agreed follow-up ownership. This is important because learning data is only useful if users trust it enough to act on it.

Recommendations from the analysis

  1. Create a weekly overdue learning exception list for managers, separated by ageing and mandatory-training risk.
  2. Track data quality issues such as missing completion dates, inactive employees, duplicated course records and inconsistent statuses.
  3. Separate compliance risk from general engagement so users do not mix two different business problems.
  4. Review courses with repeated cancellations or no-shows before assuming the problem is learner behaviour.
  5. Use feedback trends and demand patterns to prioritise course redesign and capacity planning.
  6. Automate repeat reporting where possible to reduce manual LMS export work and improve consistency.
