Case study 02 · Energy Debt Repayment Analysis

Dashboard for debt exposure, repayment behaviour and operational prioritisation.

This Power BI case study is written from the viewpoint of a data analyst supporting credit management stakeholders. It shows how repayment data can be prepared, segmented and turned into a practical reporting tool for identifying risk, monitoring collection performance and prioritising weekly follow-up.

Case study summary

Business context and reporting challenge

Credit management teams need more than a static outstanding-balance report. A total debt value does not explain whether customers are paying, whether repayment arrangements are weakening, which accounts require action, or where collection effort should be focused.

This dashboard reframes debt reporting as an operational prioritisation problem. It combines debt exposure, repayment behaviour, missed-payment indicators, segmentation and exception reporting so that managers can review performance and direct team follow-up more effectively.

Stakeholders supported

  • Credit management teams reviewing repayment performance
  • Collections and debt recovery operations prioritising follow-up
  • Finance reporting users monitoring outstanding exposure
  • Customer operations managers reviewing risk and workload
  • Business analysts supporting operational reporting improvement

Problem statement

From debt reporting to repayment action

The business problem is not simply “how much debt do we have?” The more useful question is: which accounts are most likely to need intervention, and what evidence supports that priority?

Debt exposure

Analyse how debt is distributed by customer group, risk segment, ageing band, payment method or account status.

Repayment performance

Compare expected repayment with actual repayment to highlight missed instalments, underpayment and weakening arrangements.

Operational follow-up

Turn risk indicators into an exception view so teams can prioritise accounts needing action rather than manually searching reports.

Dashboard storytelling structure

Page / area Purpose Example insight Business action
Portfolio overview Summarise total debt, account volume and key risk indicators. Total debt may remain stable while the mix shifts towards higher-risk overdue accounts. Review whether overall exposure is improving or simply changing shape.
Repayment behaviour Track whether repayment plans are on track, weakening or broken. A high-balance account may be lower risk if repayment is consistent; a smaller account may be higher risk if payments have stopped. Prioritise intervention based on behaviour, not balance alone.
Customer segmentation Group accounts by balance, payment method, risk category and repayment behaviour. Some payment methods or customer segments may show repeated missed-payment patterns. Tailor collection treatment, contact method and review frequency.
Exception follow-up Create a practical work queue for weekly credit management review. Accounts with high balance, recent missed payments and worsening ageing can be flagged for priority action. Assign follow-up, record outcome and monitor whether intervention improves repayment.

Measures and analysis ideas

  • Total outstanding debt and average balance
  • Paid versus expected amount
  • Repayment plan completion %
  • Missed payment count and missed-payment trend
  • Overdue balance and arrears ageing bucket
  • Broken arrangement indicator
  • Priority risk category and exception flag
  • Data quality checks for missing account, payment or status fields

Data professional approach

How I would build and govern the analysis

I would start by agreeing the business definitions for outstanding debt, overdue balance, expected repayment, missed payment and broken arrangement. Then I would prepare the data model around repayment transactions, balances, accounts, customers and dates, with validation checks to reconcile totals and highlight missing or inconsistent status values.

The report would be managed as a repeatable operational review: refresh checks, exception logic, agreed priority rules, stakeholder sign-off, follow-up ownership and outcome tracking. This turns the dashboard from a reporting output into a management control tool.

Recommendations from the analysis

  1. Create a weekly exception list for accounts with high balance, missed instalments or worsening ageing.
  2. Separate customers who are paying slowly from customers whose arrangements have broken.
  3. Review plan affordability when repeated underpayment appears instead of relying only on standard chasing.
  4. Use customer segment and payment method to tailor contact strategy and review frequency.
  5. Track post-contact outcomes to measure whether interventions improve repayment behaviour.
  6. Monitor data quality issues that could distort the risk view, such as missing payment dates, duplicate accounts or inconsistent status codes.
